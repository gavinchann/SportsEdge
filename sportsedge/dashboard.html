How to read these forecasts

Percentages are model estimates, not guarantees. They describe a home or away win conditional on a decisive result. NHL includes overtime and shootouts; NFL ties are excluded. Every matchup uses information available at the displayed forecast cutoff, even if the game is several days away. Injury and lineup information is unknown unless timestamped reports are supplied. These newly fitted models do not inherit the historical evaluation scores.

01 / FORECASTING

One cutoff. No future results.

Current schedules and completed scores are retrieved from ESPN scoreboards. The forecasts use a single generation cutoff. Results and availability reports observed after it cannot enter the forecast features.

Updates are saved as separate snapshots. Refreshing this page loads saved predictions; fetching new data and retraining are separate pipeline operations.

02 / TRAINING

History with explicit assumptions.

Models learn from the verified historical archive and newly retrieved older finals. New retrospective training features assume results became available seven days after game start. This is a reconstruction, not a historically observed feed.

Later model fits use more data than the original benchmark. Their future accuracy must be measured separately.

03 / AVAILABILITY

Unknown is not healthy.

Each forecast run collects timestamped team injury context. These reports are not confirmed game availability and do not change predictions. Separate game-specific inputs are supported. Missing reports remain unknown; models trained without injury examples have not learned an injury effect.

There are no dedicated starting goalie, pitcher or quarterback features yet.

04 / LIMITATIONS

Probabilities, not promises.

Pregame predictions do not include live game state, market odds, transaction costs or betting returns. Schedule changes and provider corrections can occur after collection.

NHL outcomes include overtime and shootouts. NFL estimates are conditional on a decisive result, excluding ties.
